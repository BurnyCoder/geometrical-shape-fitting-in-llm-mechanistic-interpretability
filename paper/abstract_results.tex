On \nMethod{} held-out synthetic clouds the fitter identifies the family in \accMethod{} of cases; on a shared subset it reaches \accMethodsub{} against \accBone{} for the classical pipeline and \accBtwo{} for the feature classifier, with a lower median Chamfer distance to the ground-truth shape (preregistered H1 and H2 supported). An independent audit found that the classical pipeline's selection rule could never output the null class. A post-hoc repair of it reaches \accBonefixed{}, so the advantage holds. On real activations its coarse shape class agrees with published descriptions on \hthreeAgree{}/\hthreeN{} preregistered datasets. That is above neither a permutation null (H3 not supported) nor a constant ``curve'' answer (\hthreeConst{}/\hthreeN{}), and the exact family matches on only \hthreeFine{}/\hthreeN{}. Post-hoc inspection points to curves outside the vocabulary, low-variance projections and clouds unlike any training family. Controls show that smooth ordinal processes are often labelled helix, and a preregistered held-out test against the PCA ringing artifact, while necessary, still accepts helix fits on clouds the literature describes as circles.
